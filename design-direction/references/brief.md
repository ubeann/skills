# The brief

Eight fields. Each one is a decision the model would otherwise make by averaging. Write answers in the user's product language, not design vocabulary.

## Fields and where they go

| # | Field | Answer shape | Goes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who and their job | `<who> wants to <job> on <device>, <how often>` | `PRODUCT.md` → Users |
| 2 | Surfaces | Each surface, and whether it is **selling** (landing, pricing, onboarding) or **doing** (lists, editors, settings) | `PRODUCT.md` → Platform, and a line per surface |
| 3 | Three adjectives | Each with what it means on screen: "calm: one accent colour, no badges, wide spacing" | `DESIGN.md` → Overview |
| 4 | Three nots | Concrete patterns: "not a list of text rows", "not red numbers when a target is missed" | `DESIGN.md` → Do's and Don'ts |
| 5 | The bet | The category's hidden assumption, and the opposite this product builds | `PRODUCT.md` → Positioning |
| 6 | Peak and end | The one moment lifted above the rest, and how the core flow closes | `PRODUCT.md` → Key moments |
| 7 | Hard moments | The failures, empties, waits and limits of the core flow | `PRODUCT.md` → Key moments |
| 8 | Signature element | One visual idea that is the product's own, reused to connect things, and at most two other standout ingredients | `DESIGN.md` → Overview and Components |

An adjective with no visible consequence ("modern", "clean") is a placeholder. Push until it changes a token or a rule.

## 5. The bet

Every category runs on an assumption about its users that nobody says out loud, and the leader built its business on it. Building the opposite is the move the leader cannot copy without risking what pays its bills ([video at 0:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=13s)). Write down what the top three products in the category all assume, then circle the one the leader makes money from ([video at 3:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=212s)).

Shapes the bet usually takes:

| Shape | Question to ask | Case |
|---|---|---|
| Niche depth | What would this look like built only for our narrowest real user? | Tiimo built tasks for ADHD users as coloured blocks on a timeline, sized by duration, instead of text rows ([video at 1:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA4yB_sd62Q&t=70s)) |
| Rebuild around the new capability | If our core technology were the first screen, what would that screen be? | Cal AI made the camera the home screen; typing a meal became the correction path ([video at 4:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA4yB_sd62Q&t=259s)) |
| Remove the pressure | Which feeling does the category use to force compliance? | MacroFactor dropped red numbers, broken streaks and guilt notifications, and expects users to miss targets ([video at 7:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA4yB_sd62Q&t=443s)) |
| Make the solo moment shared | Which moment done alone would mean more if others did it at the same time? | Ladder puts a whole team on the same workout on the same day ([video at 10:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA4yB_sd62Q&t=600s)) |
| Flip the effort | Does the category assume users want control, or want it done for them? | Timeleft: no bios, no swiping, no planning; you only show up to dinner ([video at 0:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=13s)) |

The bet shapes the interface, not only the marketing: a bet that changes no screen is a slogan.

## 6. Peak and end

People remember an experience by its most intense moment and its last one ([video at 8:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=486s)).

- **Peak.** Pick one point in the core flow: right after the core task succeeds, at a milestone, or where the user has invested the most. Lift it with copy, a result built in front of them, or one visual reward. One peak; several cancel out ([video at 8:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=527s)).
- **End.** Close the flow on purpose: a summary of what was done, the progress it added to, or what comes next. A flow that simply stops wastes the moment ([video at 9:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=560s)).

Neither needs animation. A summary card and one well-written line are enough.

## 7. Hard moments

AI-built screens look finished while skipping system status, undo and recovery ([video at 2:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=149s)). List the core flow's hard moments by breaking it on purpose: cut the network mid-flow, fail a payment, empty every list, hit every limit ([video at 9:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=559s)). For each, the screen answers three questions: what just happened, can I take it back, what do I do next ([video at 3:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=238s)).

The same moment gets one pattern everywhere. Undertone had two different "you've used all your sessions" screens with no count and no date; the fix was one pattern with "2 of 2 sessions used" and the exact refresh date ([video at 5:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=342s)).

## 8. Signature element

One visual idea, reused so the user connects related parts without reading. In a voting screen, the same pulsing dot marks the current period on the chart and the commit button ([video at 12:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=86PGRyQjdzQ&t=747s)). In a progress product, one visible measure of total progress does more than twenty scattered badges ([video at 6:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=377s)).

Good candidates: a shape, a way of drawing numbers, a single accent use, a recurring card form, a progress mark. It must survive on doing surfaces, so it cannot be an illustration or an animation alone.

**Ingredients.** Art directors run a chain: an insight (an unspoken truth about the user; here, the bet), the idea that makes the user feel it, then the visual decisions that carry the idea ([video at 1:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O_B_Ei5o1k4&t=117s)). Every screen makes dozens of those decisions, but a dish is remembered by two or three standout flavours ([video at 14:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O_B_Ei5o1k4&t=861s)). Name the signature element plus at most two other standout ingredients, each traced to the bet: Polaroid's campaign against AI images put handwritten ballpoint over its photos, where a clean sans would have said the same words without the feeling ([video at 7:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O_B_Ei5o1k4&t=448s)). Everything else is tuned to stay quiet beside them. Pitch the ingredients in one line as "X meets Y", two worlds rather than two products ("Dexter's Lab meets the Vikings"), so every later choice can be tested against both halves ([video at 8:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mhB9McUjvgo&t=497s)).

Decide which end the ingredients sit at. Usually they set the **feeling** for a message the copy carries; sometimes they form a **visual device** that carries the message with no copy at all, as LEGO's ads cast a dinosaur's shadow from two bricks ([video at 11:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O_B_Ei5o1k4&t=717s)). A device suits selling surfaces and the peak; doing surfaces keep the feeling and leave the words to the copy. Pick ingredients that thread across every surface the brief lists, from the app icon to the store page.
